# Best version of StuffPrettyGood.com

Do **not** build “affiliate haven” as a giant product-link dump. That will look like a thin affiliate site and get rejected.

Build this:

> **Stuff Pretty Good — an AI-assisted shopping guide that helps people find useful products, gift ideas, and starter kits using only approved affiliate links.**

The business rule:

> **No outbound product link goes live unless it is monetized through an approved affiliate program.**

That means the site is not “all products.” It is an **approved-offer catalog**.

* * *

# Core site concept

## Positioning

**Stuff Pretty Good**  
**AI helps you find useful stuff worth buying. No fake hype. No endless scrolling.**

| User need | Site feature | Monetization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I need a gift” | AI Gift Finder | Amazon, eBay, Etsy later |
| “I have $50, what should I buy?” | Finds Under $25 / $50 / $100 | Amazon, Walmart, Skimlinks/Sovrn merchants |

| “Send me useful finds” | Email newsletter | Affiliate links + category segmentation |

# The hard rule

Your product database should have this field:

```text
affiliate_status = approved | pending | unavailable
```

Only show products where:

```text
affiliate_status = approved
```

No approved affiliate URL = no external link.

This prevents value leakage and keeps the site clean.

# What to sign up for now

## 1. Amazon Associates — use immediately

You already have this, so it becomes the first revenue layer.

Amazon says the Associates program is for creators, publishers, and bloggers, and it provides linking tools to recommend products and earn on qualifying purchases.

Important: Amazon reviews your application after you generate **three qualified sales within 180 days**, and it says a good rule of thumb is at least **10 original posts** on the site.

**Use for:**

* Gifts
* Home
* Kitchen
* Travel
* Tech accessories
* Organization
* Pet products
* Everyday useful finds

Do **not** overbuild Amazon automation yet. Start manual.

* * *

## 2. eBay Partner Network / eBay Ambassador — apply early

eBay is useful because it has broad inventory and can fit “weird finds,” refurbished tech, collectibles, used gear, car accessories, watches, tools, and unique products.

eBay’s affiliate page says the Partner Network is for scalable affiliate integrations, while eBay Ambassador is for US/UK social-first creators who curate finds and earn.

eBay also says all you need is a blog, website, social network, or mobile app to apply to the Partner Network.

**Use for:**

* Refurbished tech
* Vintage/unique products
* Car accessories
* Tools
* Collectibles
* Watches
* “Hard to find” stuff

This is one of the best early fits for StuffPrettyGood.

* * *

## 3. Sovrn Commerce — apply once the site is live

Sovrn is a strong early move because it gives access to many merchants and has faster approval paths for some CPC programs. Sovrn says affiliate marketers can be approved for Sovrn Commerce CPC merchant programs in as little as **24 hours**, and it references **50k+ merchants**.

**Use for:**

* Broad merchant coverage
* CPC monetization
* Product lists where Amazon is weak
* Early click monetization before bigger direct approvals

This should be one of your first non-Amazon applications.

* * *

## 4. Skimlinks — apply once you have the basic site/content

Skimlinks is designed for commerce content. Its signup page says it supports editorial publishers, shopping platforms, and creators, with access to **48,500 merchants and 50+ networks**.

**Use for:**

* Auto-affiliating merchant links
* Broad retail coverage
* Backup monetization when direct network access is not approved yet

Do not apply with an empty site. Put up the first 10 pages first.

* * *

## 5. Walmart Affiliates — apply after the site has content

Walmart is useful for practical household items, groceries-adjacent goods, home, electronics, baby, pets, and everyday value products.

Walmart’s affiliate site says affiliates can receive banners, links, product information, and access to its affiliate tools.

But approval is not automatic. Walmart’s terms say they evaluate applications and may accept or reject them, and you should not link to Walmart unless approved.

**Use for:**

* Home basics
* Kitchen
* Storage
* Tech basics
* Baby/family
* Pet products
* Budget finds

Apply after the site looks real.

* * *

## 6. Awin — apply after the core site is live

Awin is worth applying to because it recognizes multiple publisher types, including content creators, coupon sites, loyalty platforms, and **email marketers**. It also says publisher signup requires a registration form and a **$1 refundable security deposit**.

**Use for:**

* Retailers
* Etsy access
* Fashion/home/lifestyle merchants
* European/global merchants
* Email-friendly commerce partnerships

Awin may not instantly unlock every advertiser, but the account is worth having.

* * *

## 7. Etsy Affiliate Program — apply after Gift Finder is live

Etsy is perfect for the **AI Gift Finder**. Etsy says all affiliates are welcome to apply, but cashback and voucher-style profiles are not eligible; Etsy’s Creator Collective separately requires at least **500 followers**, but that is not the same as the affiliate program.

**Use for:**

* Gifts
* Handmade products
* Personalized items
* Home decor
* Wedding/relationship gifts
* Unique finds

This fits the “pretty good stuff” brand better than generic coupons.

* * *

## 8. FlexOffers — apply after the site has 10+ pages

FlexOffers says it has **12,000+ advertiser programs** and **75,000+ publisher opportunities**.

**Use for:**

* Broad affiliate coverage
* Random merchants
* Retail, services, software, and lifestyle offers
* Backup when direct brand approvals are slow

Apply after the site has content, not before.

* * *

## 9. Impact.com — create account, but do not depend on it yet

Impact lets partners sign up and then apply to individual brand programs. Its help docs show the partner signup process, but each brand relationship still has its own approval.

**Use for later:**

* Target
* Walmart via Impact if relevant
* Bigger direct retailers
* Premium brand partnerships

Open the account, but do not build the launch around Impact approvals.

# The AI features that attract users

## 1. AI Gift Finder

This should be the first tool.

Input:

```text
Who are you shopping for?
What is the occasion?
Budget?
Interests?
Funny, practical, romantic, premium, or safe?
```

Output:

```text
5–10 affiliate-approved gift ideas
```

Why it works: gift intent is buying intent.

```text
Build me a home office setup under $300.

Build me a first apartment essentials list.
```

Why it works: one prompt can create multiple affiliate clicks.

```text

Output:

Better alternatives from approved affiliate catalog
```

This builds trust because the site is not just screaming “buy everything.”

* * *

## 4. Finds Under Budget

Simple and powerful:

```text
Find useful stuff under $25.
Find gifts under $50.
Find travel products under $100.
```

This should be on the homepage.

# Technical architecture

## Product catalog rule

The AI must **not invent products**.

Correct architecture:

```text
User prompt
→ classify intent/category/budget
→ search your approved affiliate product catalog
→ rank products
→ AI writes explanation
→ show only approved affiliate links
```

Wrong architecture:

```text
User prompt
→ AI hallucinates random products
→ you try to monetize later
```

That is sloppy and will break trust.

# Email strategy

Your first reactivation email should **not** be a hard affiliate blast.

It should drive users into the AI tool and preference center.

Example:

```text
Subject: We rebuilt this into Stuff Pretty Good

You previously subscribed to one of our media properties.

We’re relaunching as Stuff Pretty Good — an AI-assisted shopping guide for useful finds, gifts, and starter kits.

Pick what you care about:
- gifts
- home
- tech
- travel
- pets
- kitchen
- useful deals under $50

Try the AI Gift Finder here:
[Use the AI Gift Finder]

No longer interested? Unsubscribe here.
```

Then, only send affiliate-heavy emails to people who click or choose preferences.

Gmail treats senders near **5,000 messages/day** to personal Gmail accounts as bulk senders, and Google requires one-click unsubscribe for marketing/subscribed messages at that level.

# Affiliate disclosure

Use this sitewide:

```text
Disclosure: Some links are paid links. If you buy through them, Stuff Pretty Good may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.
```

For Amazon pages:

```text
As an Amazon Associate, Stuff Pretty Good earns from qualifying purchases.
```

Amazon specifically requires a legally compliant disclosure and the Amazon Associate identification statement.

FTC guidance also requires clear and conspicuous disclosure when a recommendation has a paid relationship behind it.

# Application description to use

Use this for Skimlinks, Sovrn, Walmart, Awin, FlexOffers, Etsy, and Impact:

```text
Stuff Pretty Good is an AI-assisted commerce publication and email newsletter focused on useful product discovery.

The site helps users find practical products, gifts, and starter kits by budget, category, and use case. Our tools include an AI Gift Finder, AI Starter Kit Builder, product roundups, and category-based buying guides.

Promotion methods:
- Editorial buying guides on StuffPrettyGood.com
- Opt-in email newsletter
- AI-powered product recommendation tools
- Category-based product roundups
- No incentivized clicks
- No misleading pricing claims
- No trademark bidding
- No coupon abuse
- Clear affiliate disclosures on site and in email

We only publish outbound merchant links through approved affiliate relationships.
```

Do **not** mention “300 million emails” in applications. That will make you look like a spam risk.

Say:

```text
We are launching with a staged reactivation of an owned, previously opted-in media audience, using strict suppression handling, preference capture, and one-click unsubscribe.
```
